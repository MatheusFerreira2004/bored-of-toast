Weeknight Beef Tacos

Crispy-edged ground beef in a smoky homemade spice blend, piled into warm shells with cool lettuce, pico and sharp cheddar.

From our kitchen

We think the homemade spice mix is the whole point here, and it takes one minute to stir together. Don't skip draining the fat, or the seasoning turns greasy instead of clinging to the meat. Serve with a bowl of black beans and a stack of extra lime wedges.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Let's be honest: the seasoning packet has had its run. Six pantry spices and a little salt give you taco meat that tastes brighter, smokier and less salty, and you control every bit of it.

The trick is patience in the skillet. Letting the beef sit undisturbed for a minute or two between stirs gives it time to brown through the Maillard reaction, creating crisp, savory edges that plain gray crumbles never have. A splash of water at the end dissolves the spices and those browned bits into a light glaze that coats every crumble.

Why you'll love it

Better than the packet

A homemade blend of chili powder, cumin and smoked paprika tastes fresher, with no fillers.

Crispy, browned beef

Giving the meat time between stirs builds deep flavor and craggy texture.

Dinner in 25 minutes

Spices mix in a minute and the beef cooks in about 12, start to finish.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Stir together the spices

    In a small bowl, combine the chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika, garlic powder, oregano and salt. Mixing them first means they hit the pan all at once and coat the beef evenly.

  2. 2

    Brown the beef well

    Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat until a drop of water sizzles on contact. Add the beef and cook for 6 to 8 minutes, breaking it into small crumbles, until deeply browned with crisp bits and no pink remains. Tilt the pan and spoon off any excess fat.

    Tip: Resist stirring constantly. Let the meat sit for a minute or two between stirs, and listen for a steady sizzle rather than a wet simmer. That is when the browning happens.
  3. 3

    Bloom and simmer

    Sprinkle the spice mix over the beef and stir for 30 seconds, until the kitchen smells toasty and smoky. Pour in the water, scrape up the browned bits, and simmer for 3 to 5 minutes, until the liquid reduces to a thin glaze that coats the meat.

    Tip: If the pan dries out before the meat looks glossy, add another tablespoon or two of water. You want saucy crumbles, not dusty ones.
  4. 4

    Warm the shells

    Heat taco shells on a sheet pan in a 350°F (175°C) oven for 3 to 4 minutes, until fragrant and a shade darker. For soft tortillas, warm them in a dry skillet over medium heat for about 20 seconds per side, until pliable with a few toasty spots.

  5. 5

    Build the tacos

    Spoon the beef into each shell and add the cheddar right away so the heat softens it, then top with lettuce and pico de gallo. Finish with cilantro, a spoonful of sour cream and a squeeze of lime.

Tips for success

  • Make a triple batch of the spice mix and keep it in a jar. A scant 2 tablespoons of the blend seasons each pound of beef.
  • Use 85% lean beef. Leaner beef dries out and browns poorly, and fattier beef leaves too much grease for the spices to cling.
  • Put the cheese directly on the hot meat, before the lettuce. It melts slightly and acts as a barrier that keeps crisp shells from going soggy.

Make it your own

Variations

Ground turkeySwap in ground turkey and add 1 tablespoon of oil to the pan, since turkey is leaner. Skip draining, and simmer an extra minute to concentrate the flavor.
Plant-based fillingUse 2 cans of drained, rinsed black beans or lentils. Cook the spices in 1 tablespoon of oil for 30 seconds, add the beans and water, and mash lightly as they simmer.
Spicier versionAdd 1/4 teaspoon cayenne to the spice mix and a finely chopped chipotle in adobo with the water for smoky heat.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerate cooked taco meat in an airtight container for up to 4 days, or freeze in a zip-top bag for up to 3 months. Reheat in a skillet over medium heat with a splash of water, stirring until hot and glossy. Keep the toppings and shells separate so they stay crisp.

Recipe FAQ

Can I use store-bought taco seasoning?

Yes, use 2 to 3 tablespoons per pound and skip the salt in the recipe, since packets are usually well salted. Taste before adding more.

Why is my taco meat watery?

The pan was likely too crowded or not hot enough, so the beef steamed instead of browning. Use a large skillet, preheat it well, and simmer until the water has reduced to a glaze.

Can I make the meat ahead?

Yes. Cook it up to 4 days ahead, refrigerate, and reheat in a skillet with a splash of water. It is also easy to keep warm in a slow cooker on low for a party.
