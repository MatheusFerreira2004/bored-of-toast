Custardy Cinnamon French Toast

Thick slices of brioche soaked in a rich vanilla cinnamon custard, griddled until crisp outside and creamy in the middle.

From our kitchen

We call this the reason we named the site Bored of Toast, because it is toast that is anything but boring. Our one rule is that the bread must be a day old, so we buy brioche on Friday and hide it until Sunday.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Most French toast is either dry in the middle or soggy all the way through. This version gets the best of both: a quick soak in a custard made with milk and cream, then a slow sizzle in butter until the outside turns deep golden and crisp.

The cinnamon and a little brown sugar go right into the custard, so every bite tastes like a cinnamon roll. We cook it over medium-low heat, which gives the center time to set into something soft and almost pudding-like.

Why you'll love it

Custard, not scrambled egg

Adding cream gives a silky center with no eggy flavor or streaks.

Caramelized, crisp edges

Brown sugar in the custard browns in the butter for a lightly crackly crust.

Ready in 30 minutes

No overnight soak needed, just day-old bread and a patient pan.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Heat the oven and pan

    Set the oven to 200°F (95°C) and place a wire rack on a sheet pan inside to keep finished slices warm. Put a large nonstick skillet or griddle over medium-low heat to preheat for about 3 minutes.

  2. 2

    Whisk the custard

    In a wide, shallow dish, whisk the eggs until completely smooth with no streaks of white. Add the milk, cream, brown sugar, cinnamon, vanilla, salt and nutmeg and whisk for 30 seconds until the sugar dissolves and the cinnamon is evenly suspended.

    Tip: Cinnamon likes to float on top. Give the custard a quick whisk before each new batch of bread.
  3. 3

    Soak the bread

    Lay 2 to 3 slices in the custard and soak 15 to 20 seconds per side for brioche, or up to 30 seconds per side for denser challah. The bread should feel heavy and saturated but still hold its shape when lifted. Let the excess drip off for a few seconds.

  4. 4

    Cook low and slow

    Melt 1 tablespoon butter in the skillet until foaming, then add the soaked slices. Cook 3 to 4 minutes per side until deep golden brown and the center springs back when pressed gently. If the butter starts to smoke or the outside darkens before 3 minutes, lower the heat.

    Tip: Medium-low heat is the secret. High heat browns the outside before the custard in the middle sets.
  5. 5

    Keep warm and repeat

    Move cooked slices to the rack in the oven, where they stay crisp for up to 20 minutes. Wipe any dark bits from the pan, add more butter, and repeat with the remaining bread.

  6. 6

    Serve

    Stack two slices per plate and dust with powdered sugar. Serve right away with warm maple syrup and berries.

Tips for success

  • Fresh bread soaks up custard too fast and turns mushy. If yours is fresh, dry the slices in a 300°F (150°C) oven for 8 to 10 minutes first.
  • A wire rack, not a plate, keeps the bottoms from steaming and going soft while you finish the batch.
  • Warm the maple syrup for 20 seconds in the microwave so it does not cool down your toast.

Make it your own

Variations

Orange cardamomAdd 1 teaspoon orange zest and 1/4 teaspoon ground cardamom to the custard in place of the nutmeg.
Cinnamon sugar crustSprinkle each side with a mix of 1/4 cup sugar and 1 teaspoon cinnamon right after it hits the pan.
Dairy-freeUse oat milk and full-fat canned coconut milk instead of the milk and cream, and cook in coconut oil or vegan butter.

Storage & reheating

Cool leftovers completely, then refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days or freeze in a single layer, then bag, for up to 2 months. Reheat in a toaster or a 350°F (175°C) oven for 8 to 10 minutes until hot and crisp again.

Recipe FAQ

What is the best bread for French toast?

Brioche or challah sliced about 1 inch thick gives the most custardy result. Thick-cut Texas toast or a sturdy sourdough also work but need a slightly longer soak.

Why is my French toast soggy in the middle?

Either the heat was too high, so the outside browned before the center cooked, or the bread was too fresh. Try medium-low heat and day-old bread.

Can I make the custard ahead?

Yes. Whisk it the night before and refrigerate it covered. Whisk again before using since the cinnamon settles.
