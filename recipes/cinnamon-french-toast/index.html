Custardy Cinnamon French Toast

Thick slices of day-old brioche soaked in a vanilla-cinnamon custard, then cooked slowly in butter until crisp outside and pudding-soft within.

From our kitchen

If any recipe explains our name, it is this one: toast, but far from boring. Day-old bread is the one thing we won't bend on, because fresh brioche collapses into mush in the custard. Serve it with warm maple syrup, berries and crisp bacon on the side.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Soggy centers and scorched outsides are the two ways French toast goes wrong, and both come down to heat and bread. Fix those, and you get slices that are crisp and caramelized on the surface with a center like bread pudding.

Day-old brioche or challah is slightly drier, so it drinks up more custard without falling apart. The rich mix of eggs, milk and cream needs gentle heat to set, since egg proteins firm up gradually, and medium-low heat gives the middle time to cook before the sugar and butter on the surface go past golden to burnt.

Why you'll love it

Truly custardy centers

A rich egg, milk and cream custard soaks deep and sets like bread pudding.

Browned, never burnt

Medium-low heat gives crisp, caramelized edges while the middle cooks through.

Everyone eats together

A warm oven holds finished slices crisp so the whole batch hits the table hot.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Preheat oven and skillet

    Heat the oven to 200°F (95°C) and set a wire rack on a sheet pan inside to hold finished slices. Place a large nonstick skillet or griddle over medium-low heat for about 3 minutes, so it heats evenly without hot spots.

  2. 2

    Whisk the custard

    In a wide, shallow dish, whisk the eggs until completely smooth with no streaks of white, which would otherwise cook into patches of plain egg. Add the milk, cream, brown sugar, cinnamon, vanilla, salt and nutmeg and whisk for 30 seconds, until the sugar dissolves and the cinnamon is evenly suspended.

    Tip: Cinnamon floats and clumps on top of the custard. Give it a quick whisk before each new batch so every slice gets its share.
  3. 3

    Soak the bread

    Lay 2 or 3 slices in the custard and soak 15 to 20 seconds per side for brioche, or up to 30 seconds per side for denser challah. The bread should feel heavy and saturated but still hold its shape when lifted. Let the excess drip off for a few seconds so the surface browns instead of steaming.

  4. 4

    Cook low and slow

    Melt 1 tablespoon of butter in the skillet until it foams and smells nutty, then add the soaked slices. Cook 3 to 4 minutes per side, until deep golden brown and the center springs back when pressed gently. If the butter starts to smoke or the outside darkens before 3 minutes, lower the heat.

    Tip: Unsure if the middle is set? Press the center with a spatula. Liquid custard seeping out means it needs another minute per side on slightly lower heat.
  5. 5

    Keep warm and repeat

    Move cooked slices to the rack in the oven, where they stay crisp for up to 20 minutes. Wipe any dark bits from the pan with a paper towel, add more butter, and repeat with the remaining bread.

  6. 6

    Serve

    Stack two slices per plate and dust with powdered sugar. Serve right away with warm maple syrup and fresh berries.

Tips for success

  • No day-old bread? Dry fresh slices on a rack in a 300°F (150°C) oven for 8 to 10 minutes, flipping once. They should feel dry to the touch but not toasted.
  • Whisk the custard the night before and refrigerate it covered. Whisk again in the morning, since the cinnamon settles overnight.
  • Wiping the pan between batches matters. Browned milk solids and sugar left behind will burn and leave bitter dark spots on the next slices.

Make it your own

Variations

Orange and cardamomSwap the nutmeg for 1/4 teaspoon ground cardamom and add the finely grated zest of 1 orange to the custard.
Dairy-freeUse 1 1/2 cups of full-fat oat milk or canned coconut milk in place of the milk and cream, and cook in coconut oil or vegan butter. Soak a few seconds less, as thinner custards absorb faster.
Stuffed with cream cheeseSandwich 2 thinner slices with sweetened cream cheese and a little jam before soaking. Cook a minute longer per side on low so the filling warms through.

Storage & reheating

Cool leftovers completely, then refrigerate in an airtight container for up to 3 days. To freeze, lay slices on a sheet pan until solid, then transfer to a freezer bag for up to 2 months. Reheat in a toaster or a 350°F (175°C) oven for 8 to 10 minutes until hot and crisp.

Recipe FAQ

Why is my French toast soggy in the middle?

The heat was probably too high, so the outside browned before the custard set, or the bread was too fresh and soaked too long. Use day-old bread, stick to medium-low heat, and press the center to check.

Can I use regular sandwich bread?

Yes, but it is thinner and softer, so soak it just 5 to 10 seconds per side and expect a less custardy center. Thick-cut or day-old bread gives the best result.

Can I make this ahead for a crowd?

Cook all the slices, then hold them on the rack in the 200°F (95°C) oven for up to 20 minutes. For longer, cool, freeze, and reheat in a 350°F (175°C) oven.
