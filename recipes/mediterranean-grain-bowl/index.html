Mediterranean Grain Bowl

Nutty quinoa, cumin-roasted chickpeas that actually crunch, briny olives and feta, all tied together with a sharp oregano-lemon dressing.

From our kitchen

We think this bowl is at its best on day two, once the quinoa has soaked up a little dressing, so make a full batch on Sunday. The one thing not to skip is drying the chickpeas properly. Wet chickpeas steam instead of roast, and you lose the crunch that makes the whole bowl work.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Texture is what separates a great grain bowl from a sad desk lunch. Most fall flat because everything in them has the same soft texture and nothing is seasoned boldly enough. This one fixes both problems with chickpeas roasted until they rattle, cool crisp cucumber, salty olives and feta, and a dressing with real acidity.

The trick to crunchy chickpeas is getting the water off the surface before they hit the oven. Any moisture has to evaporate before the outsides can brown, so a thorough towel dry means the high heat goes straight into crisping rather than steaming. Letting the quinoa rest covered off the heat does something similar for the base: the last of the steam finishes the grains gently, so they come out separate and fluffy instead of wet.

Real crunch in every bite

Hot-roasted, bone-dry chickpeas stay crisp against the soft quinoa and juicy tomatoes.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Roast the chickpeas

    Heat the oven to 425°F (220°C). Toss the well-dried chickpeas with the olive oil, cumin, smoked paprika and a pinch of salt, then spread them in a single layer on a sheet pan. Roast for 20 to 25 minutes, shaking the pan halfway, until they are deep golden, smell toasty and rattle when you shake the pan.

    Tip: Roll the chickpeas in a clean kitchen towel and pull off any loose skins as you go. Dry chickpeas and fewer skins mean a crisper result, and a crowded pan will steam them, so use a large sheet.
  2. 2

    Cook the quinoa

    Combine the rinsed quinoa and water or broth in a saucepan and bring to a boil over high heat. Cover, turn the heat to low and simmer for 15 minutes, until the liquid is absorbed and you can see little spiral tails on the grains. Take the pan off the heat, leave it covered for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork.

    Tip: Rinsing matters. Quinoa is coated in saponins, which taste bitter and soapy, so rub it under cold water in a fine-mesh strainer until the water runs clear.
  3. 3

    Prep the vegetables

    While everything cooks, dice the cucumber, halve the tomatoes and slice the red onion as thinly as you can. Soak the onion in cold water for 5 minutes, then drain. The soak rinses away some of the sulfur compounds, so it stays crunchy but loses its harsh bite.

  4. 4

    Make the dressing

    Whisk the extra-virgin olive oil, lemon juice and dried oregano with a good pinch of salt and a few grinds of pepper until the dressing looks slightly thickened and cloudy. Taste it on a piece of cucumber: it should be sharp and a little salty, since the quinoa will soften it.

  5. 5

    Build the bowls

    Divide the warm or room-temperature quinoa among 4 bowls. Arrange the cucumber, tomatoes, onion, olives and chickpeas in sections on top, scatter over the feta and parsley, and drizzle with the dressing just before eating so the chickpeas keep their crunch.

    Tip: Toss a spoonful of dressing into the quinoa itself before topping. Seasoning the base means the bottom of the bowl tastes as good as the top.

Tips for success

  • Season the quinoa cooking liquid with about 1/2 teaspoon of salt. Grains absorb salt as they cook, which seasons them all the way through in a way that sprinkling at the end cannot.
  • If your chickpeas soften after a day, spread them on a sheet pan and give them 5 minutes at 400°F (200°C). They crisp right back up once the absorbed moisture cooks off.
  • For meal prep, pack the dressing in a small separate jar and add the feta on the day. Feta weeps liquid in the fridge and will make the vegetables soggy if it sits on them for days.

Make it your own

Variations

Swap in farroUse 1 cup farro instead of quinoa. Simmer it in plenty of salted water for 25 to 30 minutes until chewy, then drain. Note that farro contains gluten.
Add grilled chickenTop each bowl with about 4 ounces of sliced grilled chicken or salmon for a heartier dinner. Use the same dressing as a quick marinade for 30 minutes.
Make it veganLeave out the feta and add a big spoonful of hummus or diced avocado for creaminess, plus an extra squeeze of lemon.

Storage & reheating

Store the quinoa and vegetables, without dressing or chickpeas, in airtight containers in the fridge for up to 4 days. Keep the chickpeas in a loosely covered container at room temperature for up to 2 days so they stay crisp, and keep the dressing in a jar in the fridge, shaking it well before using. Eat the bowls cold or at room temperature; if you want warm quinoa, microwave it for 45 to 60 seconds before adding the toppings.

Recipe FAQ

Can I use canned chickpeas straight from the can?

Yes, but drain, rinse and dry them thoroughly first. Wet chickpeas will steam in the oven and come out soft rather than crisp.

Why is my quinoa mushy?

Usually too much liquid or skipping the covered rest. Stick to a 1 to 2 ratio, keep the heat low, and let it sit off the heat for 5 minutes before fluffing.

How far ahead can I make it?

Cook the quinoa, prep the vegetables and mix the dressing up to 4 days ahead. Roast the chickpeas no more than 2 days ahead, and assemble each bowl just before eating.
