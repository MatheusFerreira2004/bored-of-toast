Warm Garlic Sesame Edamame

Steamed edamame tossed with sesame oil, a little garlic and flaky salt. Ready in 10 minutes, with about 14 g protein and 6 g fiber per bowl.

From our kitchen

A warm bowl of edamame is a good snack for slow afternoons. It takes ten minutes, and eating it pod by pod is almost relaxing.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

A bag of frozen edamame is one of the easiest high-protein snacks there is. Steam the pods, toss them with a little sesame oil and garlic, sprinkle with salt and eat them warm, one pod at a time.

Popping the beans out of the pods slows you down, which is often helpful when your appetite is small. Each bowl has about 14 g of plant protein.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Steam

    Bring an inch of water to a boil in a pot with a steamer basket, or use a pot of boiling salted water. Cook the edamame for 5 to 6 minutes, until hot and tender. Drain well.

  2. 2

    Warm the garlic

    In the same pot, warm the sesame oil and garlic over low heat for 30 seconds, just until fragrant.

    Tip: Keep the heat low; garlic turns bitter if it browns.
  3. 3

    Toss

    Add the edamame and tamari and toss to coat.

  4. 4

    Serve warm

    Tip into two small bowls and sprinkle with flaky salt and sesame seeds. Squeeze the beans out of the pods with your teeth and discard the pods.

Tips for success

  • Microwave option: cook the pods in a covered bowl with 2 tablespoons water for 4 to 5 minutes.
  • Shelled edamame works too; toss and eat with a spoon.
  • Put out a small empty bowl for the pods.
  • Small-portion tip: one bowl is a full snack. The second keeps well in the fridge and is good cold.

Make it your own

Variations

Chili limeToss with lime zest and a pinch of chili flakes instead of garlic.
Plain saltedSkip the garlic and tamari; just salt.
Shelled bowlUse shelled edamame and add diced cucumber for a quick salad.
Make it gentlerUse shelled edamame, skip the garlic and season with a little salt only.
Protein boostServe alongside a soft-boiled egg for about 6 g more protein.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerate cooked edamame in a covered container for up to 3 days. Eat cold or rewarm briefly in the microwave.

Recipe FAQ

Do you eat the pods?

No. The pods are tough; squeeze the beans out and discard the shells.

Is it gluten-free?

Yes, with tamari or a gluten-free soy sauce.

Fresh or frozen?

Frozen is easier to find and already blanched, so it cooks faster.
