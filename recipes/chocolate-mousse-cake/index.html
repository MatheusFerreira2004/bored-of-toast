No-Bake Chocolate Mousse Cake

A crunchy cookie crust under thick, silky dark chocolate mousse that sets in the fridge while you get on with your day.

Cook + chill6 h 5 min
Total6 h 35 min
Serves10

From our kitchen

This is our pick when you need an impressive dessert but your oven is busy or it is too hot to turn it on. Use a chocolate you would happily eat on its own, since there is nothing to hide behind. Tart raspberries on top are not just decoration; they cut the richness beautifully.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

A dessert this dramatic has no business being this easy. There are only a handful of ingredients, the stove is on for about five minutes, and the fridge does the rest of the work overnight. What you get is a tall, glossy slab of mousse that slices cleanly over a crisp chocolate cookie base.

The mousse sets because of two things working together: air and cocoa butter. Whipping cream to soft peaks traps tiny air bubbles in a network of fat, and as the chocolate cools in the fridge, its cocoa butter firms up and locks those bubbles in place. That is also why temperature matters so much when you fold. Chocolate that is too warm melts the fat in the cream and the air collapses, while chocolate that is too cold seizes into little flecks.

Why you'll love it

No baking at all

Just a brief warm-up of cream, then the fridge sets it into something sliceable.

Rich but still light

Folded whipped cream gives an airy texture that does not feel heavy after dinner.

Made the day before

It needs at least 6 hours to set, which makes it an ideal make-ahead party dessert.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Press the cookie crust

    Stir the crushed cookie crumbs with the melted butter until the mixture looks like wet sand and holds together when squeezed. Press it firmly and evenly into the bottom of a 9-inch (23 cm) springform pan, using the flat base of a measuring cup to pack it tight. Refrigerate while you make the mousse so the butter firms up.

  2. 2

    Melt the chocolate

    Heat 1/2 cup of the cream in a small saucepan over medium heat until it is steaming with small bubbles around the edge, about 2 minutes, but not boiling. Pour it over the chopped chocolate, leave it alone for 2 minutes, then stir slowly from the center outward until smooth and glossy. Stir in the salt and let it cool until it feels just barely warm on your lip, about 10 to 15 minutes.

    Tip: If the chocolate turns grainy or stiff, whisk in hot cream a teaspoon at a time until it loosens. If it is still warm when you fold, the whipped cream will melt and the mousse will not set.
  3. 3

    Whip the cream

    Beat the remaining 1 1/2 cups of cold cream with the powdered sugar and vanilla on medium-high speed until soft peaks form, about 2 to 3 minutes. When you lift the whisk, the peaks should hold their shape but gently flop over at the tip. Stop there, since stiffer cream is harder to fold and can turn the mousse grainy.

    Tip: Chill the bowl and beaters for 10 minutes first. Cold fat traps air more easily, so the cream whips faster and holds more volume.
  4. 4

    Fold it together

    Stir about one third of the whipped cream into the chocolate to loosen it. Add the rest in two additions, using a flexible spatula to cut down through the middle, sweep along the bottom and turn the mixture over, rotating the bowl as you go. Stop as soon as no white streaks remain.

  5. 5

    Chill until set

    Scrape the mousse onto the crust and smooth the top with an offset spatula. Cover the pan with plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 6 hours, or overnight, until the mousse is firm to a gentle touch and no longer wobbles in the center.

  6. 6

    Decorate and slice

    Run a thin knife around the edge and release the springform ring. Pile the raspberries on top and finish with chocolate curls or a dusting of cocoa powder. For clean slices, dip a long knife in hot water, wipe it dry, and repeat between every cut.

Tips for success

  • Chop the chocolate finely and evenly. Small pieces melt completely from the residual heat of the cream, so you avoid lumps and never need to put the chocolate back on the stove.
  • Choose chocolate in the 60 to 70% range. Higher percentages have less sugar and more cocoa solids, which makes the mousse firmer and more bitter, and they are more likely to seize when cream is added.
  • Make chocolate curls by warming a bar in your hands for a minute, then dragging a vegetable peeler along its long side. A slightly warm bar curls instead of shattering.

Make it your own

Variations

Mocha mousse cakeDissolve 2 teaspoons of instant espresso powder in the hot cream before pouring it over the chocolate. It deepens the chocolate without tasting strongly of coffee.
Graham cracker crustUse about 1 1/2 cups of graham cracker crumbs with the same melted butter plus 2 tablespoons of sugar for a lighter, honeyed base.
Orange chocolateAdd the finely grated zest of 1 orange to the whipping cream and top the cake with segmented oranges instead of raspberries.

Storage & reheating

Keep the cake covered in the fridge for up to 4 days, adding the raspberries shortly before serving so they do not bleed into the mousse. To freeze, wrap the undecorated cake tightly in plastic wrap and foil and freeze for up to 1 month. Thaw it overnight in the fridge and serve cold; do not microwave it, as the mousse will melt.

Recipe FAQ

Can I use milk chocolate?

You can, but milk chocolate has less cocoa butter and more sugar, so the mousse will be softer and sweeter. Use 12 ounces instead of 10 to help it set, and skip the powdered sugar.

Why didn't my mousse set?

Usually the chocolate was too warm when folded, which deflated the cream, or the cream was underwhipped. Give it the full 6 hours too. Mousse that is still soft after that can be frozen for 2 hours and served semifreddo-style.

Can I make it without a springform pan?

Yes. Use a 9-inch pie dish and serve it straight from the dish, or line a regular cake pan with a plastic wrap sling so you can lift it out once set.
