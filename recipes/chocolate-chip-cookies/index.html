Chewy Chocolate Chip Cookies

Melted-butter cookies with crackly golden edges, soft centers that bend without breaking, and pools of dark chocolate under flaky salt.

Prep15 min
Cook12 min

Makes24

From our kitchen

If you can spare the time, chill the scooped dough. Even 30 minutes gives you thicker cookies with a toffee edge, and a full day is better still. We like them best still warm, with a glass of cold milk.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Melted butter is the whole secret here. Creamed butter traps air and gives you a cakey cookie, but melted butter coats the flour and limits gluten development, so you get a dense, chewy center that bends instead of crumbling. A generous share of brown sugar helps too, since its molasses holds on to moisture.

The other lever is time. When the scooped dough rests in the fridge, the flour fully absorbs the liquid and the butter firms up, so the cookies spread less and brown more deeply. You can bake right away and still be happy, but a rest turns good cookies into ones people ask about.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Heat the oven

    Heat the oven to 350°F (175°C) with a rack in the center and line two baking sheets with parchment paper. Baking one sheet at a time on the middle rack gives the most even color.

  2. 2

    Whisk butter and sugars

    Whisk the melted, slightly cooled butter with the brown and granulated sugars for about 1 minute, until the mixture looks smooth and glossy rather than grainy. Add the eggs and vanilla and whisk another 30 to 45 seconds, until the batter lightens in color and turns thick and ribbony. Warm butter can scramble eggs, so make sure it feels just warm to the touch.

  3. 3

    Fold in the dry ingredients

    Add the flour, baking soda and salt. Stir with a spatula until just combined and no dry streaks remain, then fold in the chocolate. The dough will be soft and shiny.

    Tip: Stop mixing as soon as the flour disappears. Every extra stir develops gluten, which makes cookies tough instead of chewy.
  4. 4

    Scoop and chill

    Scoop 2-tablespoon balls of dough onto the sheets, leaving 2 inches (5 cm) between them. For the best texture, chill the scooped dough for at least 30 minutes or up to 24 hours. Chilled dough spreads less and bakes up thicker, with a more caramelized flavor.

  5. 5

    Bake

    Bake one sheet at a time for 10 to 12 minutes, until the edges are golden brown and set but the centers look puffy, pale and slightly underdone. Your kitchen should smell like brown butter and toffee.

    Tip: Cookies look raw in the middle when they are actually ready. Trust the golden edges and pull them. Carryover heat on the pan finishes the centers.
  6. 6

    Finish and cool

    Sprinkle with flaky salt right away, while the tops are still tacky enough to hold it. Cool on the pan for 5 minutes so the centers set, then move to a wire rack to cool completely or eat warm.

Tips for success

  • Measure flour by spooning it into the cup and leveling it, or weigh 375 g. Scooping straight from the bag packs in extra flour and gives you dry, puffy cookies that barely spread.
  • For round, bakery-style cookies, set a large glass or cookie cutter over each cookie right out of the oven and swirl it in small circles to tuck the edges in.
  • Chopping a bar of chocolate beats chips. The shards and dust melt into streaks and puddles, while chips are made with stabilizers to hold their shape.

Make it your own

Variations

Brown butterCook the butter in a light-colored pan over medium heat until it foams and smells nutty with brown flecks, about 5 minutes. Cool for 10 minutes before whisking with the sugars.
Mixed chocolateUse half dark and half milk chocolate for a sweeter, deeper chocolate flavor, keeping the total at 2 cups.
Toasted nutsReplace 1/2 cup of the chocolate with toasted, chopped walnuts or pecans for crunch.

Storage & reheating

Keep baked cookies in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 5 days, and warm them at 300°F (150°C) for 3 to 4 minutes to bring back the soft centers. Freeze scooped dough balls in a zip-top bag for up to 3 months and bake straight from frozen, adding 1 to 2 minutes.

Recipe FAQ

Why did my cookies spread flat?

Usually the butter was too hot when mixed, the dough was not chilled, or the flour was under-measured. Let the butter cool until just warm, chill the scoops for 30 minutes, and weigh the flour if you can.

Can I use salted butter?

Yes. Cut the fine salt down to 1/2 teaspoon so the cookies do not taste overly salty, especially with the flaky salt on top.

Can I bake both sheets at once?

You can, on the upper and lower thirds of the oven, but swap and rotate the sheets halfway through and expect to add a minute or two. One at a time gives more even browning.
