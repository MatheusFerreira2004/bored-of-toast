Grilled Chicken Avocado Salad

Paprika-rubbed chicken, seared hard and sliced warm over arugula, with cool avocado and a honey-lemon dressing that shakes up in seconds.

From our kitchen

We think this salad is at its best when the chicken goes on still warm, so the greens wilt just slightly at the edges. Make a double batch of the dressing, since it keeps all week and wakes up roasted vegetables too. The one step not to skip is the rest after searing.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Why do so many chicken salads taste like a punishment? Usually it is dry, pale chicken and a dressing that slides right off the leaves. Here the breasts get a smoky paprika rub and a hard, undisturbed sear, so you get a browned crust and a juicy center in about 12 minutes.

The trick is a dry surface. Patting the chicken with paper towels removes the moisture that would otherwise have to boil off before the Maillard reaction can start, which is why a wet breast steams and turns gray. While it rests, the honey in the dressing helps hold the oil and lemon in a creamy emulsion that actually clings to the arugula.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Dry and season the chicken

    Pat the chicken breasts thoroughly dry with paper towels. If one end is much thicker, pound it to an even 3/4-inch thickness between two sheets of plastic wrap. Rub all over with the olive oil, then season both sides with the smoked paprika, garlic powder and salt.

    Tip: Even thickness is the whole game with breasts. A thin tail and a fat middle guarantees one part dries out while the other is still pink.
  2. 2

    Sear without touching

    Heat a grill pan or skillet over medium-high heat until a drop of water hisses on contact. Lay in the chicken and leave it alone for 5 to 6 minutes, until it releases easily and the underside is deep golden with a toasty paprika smell. Flip and cook another 5 to 6 minutes, until the thickest part reads 165°F (74°C).

    Tip: If the chicken sticks when you try to flip it, wait another minute. Once the crust forms, it releases on its own.
  3. 3

    Rest, then slice

    Move the chicken to a cutting board and let it rest for 5 minutes. As it cools slightly, the muscle fibers relax and hold onto their juices instead of flooding the board. Slice against the grain into 1/2-inch strips.

  4. 4

    Shake the dressing

    Add the extra-virgin olive oil, lemon juice, honey and a pinch of salt and pepper to a small jar. Screw on the lid and shake hard for 15 seconds, until the dressing turns pale, cloudy and slightly thick. Taste and add more salt or lemon if it needs brightness.

  5. 5

    Toss and build the bowls

    Toss the greens and cherry tomatoes with half the dressing until the leaves look glossy, and divide between two bowls. Top with the warm sliced chicken and fresh avocado slices. Drizzle with the remaining dressing and scatter the toasted sesame seeds over the top.

Tips for success

  • Salt the chicken up to a day ahead and leave it uncovered in the fridge. The salt draws out moisture, dissolves into it and is reabsorbed, seasoning the meat deeper and drying the surface for a better crust.
  • Toast the sesame seeds in a dry skillet over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes, shaking the pan, until golden and fragrant. Raw seeds taste flat by comparison.
  • If your avocado is underripe, slice it thin and toss it with a little of the dressing first. The acid and salt soften its firmness and bring out more flavor.
  • Small-portion tip: serve half a portion and save the rest for later. A half bowl still counts.

Make it your own

Variations

Swap in halloumiReplace the chicken with 8 oz sliced halloumi, seared 2 minutes per side over medium-high heat. Skip the salt in the rub, since the cheese is already salty.
Use chicken thighsBoneless thighs stay juicier and forgive overcooking. Cook 6 to 7 minutes per side, to 175°F (80°C).
Roll it into wrapsChop everything smaller and roll into two large tortillas with the dressing on the side, so the wrap stays crisp until lunch.
Make it gentlerShred the chicken instead of slicing it, and leave out raw onion or a strong dressing.
Protein boostAdd a soft-boiled egg or a handful of shelled edamame.

Storage & reheating

Keep the cooked chicken, washed greens and dressing in separate airtight containers in the fridge for up to 3 days. The dressing will firm up when cold, so let it sit 10 minutes and shake again. Eat the chicken cold, or warm it in a skillet over medium-low heat for 2 to 3 minutes, and slice the avocado fresh.

Recipe FAQ

How do I know the chicken is done without a thermometer?

Cut into the thickest part. The juices should run clear and the meat should be opaque all the way through with no pink. A thermometer is still the best $15 you can spend, since it takes the guessing out of lean breasts.

Can I make the dressing ahead?

Yes. It keeps in a sealed jar in the fridge for up to a week. The olive oil solidifies when cold, so let it warm up for a few minutes and shake it well before using.

Why did my chicken turn out dry?

It was likely cooked past 165°F or sliced right away without resting. Pull it as soon as the thickest part hits temperature, and pound uneven breasts so the thin end does not overcook.
