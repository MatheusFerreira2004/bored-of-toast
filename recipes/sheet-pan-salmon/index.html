Honey Dijon Sheet Pan Salmon

Salmon fillets brushed with honey, Dijon and garlic, roasted hot under lemon and herbs until glossy and just flaking.

Prep10 min
Cook15 min
Total25 min
Serves4

From our kitchen

We reach for this on nights when we want dinner to feel a little special without any real effort. Pull the fish at 125 to 130°F, because carryover heat finishes the job and overcooked salmon is the only way to ruin it. Serve it with rice to catch the pan glaze.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Nervous about cooking fish at home? A hot sheet pan is the most forgiving method you can pick: no flipping, no fillets welded to a skillet, and no lingering smell. You brush, you top, you roast, and dinner is ready in about 25 minutes.

The glaze does two jobs. Honey browns quickly at 400°F, giving the top a sticky, caramelized edge, while the Dijon acts as an emulsifier that keeps the oil and honey clinging to the fish instead of sliding off into the pan. The lemon slices and herbs laid on top shield the flesh and release steam, so the center stays silky.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Heat the oven

    Preheat the oven to 400°F (200°C) with a rack in the center, and line a rimmed sheet pan with parchment paper. Give the oven the full 15 minutes to heat so the glaze starts sizzling the moment the pan goes in.

  2. 2

    Season the fillets

    Pat the salmon very dry with paper towels, pressing gently so the surface feels tacky rather than wet. Arrange the fillets on the pan with about an inch between them so hot air can circulate, then season both sides with salt and pepper.

    Tip: Take the salmon out of the fridge 15 minutes before cooking. A less chilled center cooks at the same pace as the edges, so you avoid dry ends and a raw middle.
  3. 3

    Brush on the glaze

    In a small bowl, whisk the olive oil, honey, Dijon and garlic until the mixture looks smooth and slightly thickened, about 20 seconds. Brush it generously over the tops and sides of each fillet, using all of it.

  4. 4

    Top and roast

    Lay the lemon slices and herb sprigs over the salmon. Roast for 12 to 15 minutes, until the glaze bubbles at the edges, the flesh turns from translucent to opaque pink, and the thickest part flakes with a fork. An instant-read thermometer should read 125 to 130°F (52 to 54°C) for medium.

    Tip: Seeing white beads on the surface? That is albumin, a protein squeezed out as the fish overcooks. Next time, pull it a minute or two sooner.
  5. 5

    Rest and serve

    Let the salmon rest on the pan for 2 minutes so the juices settle and the center finishes cooking. Spoon any glaze pooled on the parchment over the top, then serve with rice, roasted vegetables or a green salad.

Tips for success

  • For a deeper, lacquered top, switch the oven to broil for the final 1 to 2 minutes and stay at the door. Honey goes from amber to burnt in under a minute.
  • Fillet thickness matters more than weight. Thin tail pieces can be done in 9 or 10 minutes, so start checking early and pull pieces as they finish.
  • Skin-on fillets lift cleanly off the parchment, leaving the skin behind. Slide a thin spatula between the flesh and skin for a boneless, skinless serving.

Make it your own

Variations

Maple and whole-grain mustardSwap the honey for maple syrup and use whole-grain mustard for a pleasantly seedy texture. The roasting time stays the same.
Spicy glazeWhisk 1 to 2 teaspoons of sriracha or 1/2 teaspoon of red pepper flakes into the glaze and use lime slices instead of lemon.
Whole-meal sheet panToss asparagus or green beans with a little oil and salt and add them around the fish. They roast in the same 12 to 15 minutes.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for up to 2 days. Eat them cold, flaked over salad, or reheat covered at 275°F (135°C) for about 10 minutes so the fish warms through without drying out.

Recipe FAQ

How do I know when salmon is done without a thermometer?

Press a fork into the thickest part and twist gently. If it separates into flakes and the center has just turned opaque with a hint of deeper pink, it is ready. If it resists, give it 2 more minutes.

Can I use frozen salmon?

Yes, but thaw it first, ideally overnight in the fridge. Roasting from frozen leaves the outside overdone before the middle cooks, and the glaze slides off the icy surface.

Why did my glaze burn?

Honey sugars scorch quickly, especially near the pan edges or under a broiler. Keep the pan on the center rack, use parchment, and if your oven runs hot, check at 10 minutes.
