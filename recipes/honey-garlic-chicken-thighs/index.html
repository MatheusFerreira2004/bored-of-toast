Sticky Honey Garlic Chicken Thighs

Golden-seared thighs simmered in honey, soy and a heap of garlic until the sauce turns glossy, sticky and dark as lacquer.

DF
Prep10 min
Cook25 min
Total35 min
Serves4
Jump to recipe Save

Photo: Marvin Sacdalan on Pexels

From our kitchen

Serve these over plenty of steamed rice so none of the glaze goes to waste, with some quick stir-fried broccoli or cucumber salad on the side. We would take thighs over breasts here every time, since they stay juicy through the simmer. Do not rush the sear.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Here is a bold claim: boneless thighs are the most forgiving cut in your grocery store, and this sticky glaze proves it. The chicken gets a hard sear, a short simmer in honey, soy and six cloves of garlic, and a last-minute thickening that leaves every piece shiny and coated. It tastes like takeout and costs a fraction of it.

Thighs work so well because they carry more fat and connective tissue than breasts, so they stay moist even at 175°F, where collagen starts softening into gelatin. The cornstarch slurry goes in last for a reason: starch granules only swell and thicken once the sauce comes near a boil, so a brief, bubbling simmer turns thin liquid into a glaze that clings.

Why you'll love it

One skillet, 35 minutes

Searing, simmering and glazing all happen in the same pan, which also keeps the browned bits in the sauce.

Hard to overcook

Thighs stay tender and juicy well past the point where breasts would turn dry.

Pantry sauce, big flavor

Honey, soy sauce, vinegar and garlic are probably in your kitchen already.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Dry and season the thighs

    Pat the thighs very dry on both sides with paper towels. Season all over with the salt, pepper and smoked paprika. Dry chicken browns faster and splatters less, so take the extra minute here.

    Tip: If you have 15 minutes, let the seasoned thighs sit at room temperature. The salt starts to season the meat and the surface dries a little more for a deeper sear.
  2. 2

    Mix the sauce and slurry

    In a small bowl, whisk the honey, soy sauce, vinegar, garlic and red pepper flakes, if using, until the honey dissolves. In a separate cup, stir the cornstarch into the water until completely smooth with no lumps, and set it aside.

  3. 3

    Sear until deep golden

    Heat the oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat until it shimmers and moves like water. Lay the thighs in smooth side down; they should sizzle loudly. Cook without moving for 5 to 6 minutes, until deep golden brown and releasing easily, then flip and cook 4 to 5 minutes more.

    Tip: Use a 12-inch skillet or work in two batches. Crowded chicken traps steam and turns pale instead of browning.
  4. 4

    Simmer in the sauce

    Lower the heat to medium and pour the sauce around the chicken, scraping up the browned bits from the pan. Let it bubble gently for 5 to 6 minutes, turning the thighs once, until the chicken reaches 175°F (80°C) inside and the sauce has reduced by about a third. The garlic should smell sweet and mellow, not raw.

    Tip: Watch the heat closely. Honey scorches quickly, so if the sauce starts to smell bitter or darken at the edges, turn it down right away.
  5. 5

    Thicken the glaze

    Stir the cornstarch slurry again, since the starch settles fast, and pour it into the pan. Simmer for 1 to 2 minutes, spooning the sauce over the chicken, until it turns thick, glossy and coats the back of a spoon.

  6. 6

    Rest, garnish and serve

    Take the pan off the heat and let it sit for 2 minutes so the glaze sets slightly as it cools. Scatter the green onions and sesame seeds over the top. Serve over steamed rice, spooning the extra sauce over everything.

Tips for success

  • Mince the garlic by hand rather than using a press. Pressed garlic releases more of its harsh compounds and can taste sharp even after simmering, while chopped garlic stays sweeter.
  • If the glaze gets too thick or gluey, loosen it with 1 to 2 tablespoons of hot water. If it is too thin, simmer another minute; do not add more cornstarch without mixing it into cold water first, or it will clump.
  • Mix the sauce and slurry up to 3 days ahead and keep them in separate sealed jars in the fridge. Dinner becomes a 20-minute sear-and-simmer.

Make it your own

Variations

Use chicken breastsSlice 2 lb breasts into 1-inch strips, sear 2 to 3 minutes per side, and simmer in the sauce only until they reach 165°F (74°C), about 3 to 4 minutes.
Make it gluten-freeUse tamari instead of soy sauce. Everything else in the recipe is already gluten-free, including the cornstarch.
Add ginger and limeGrate 1 tablespoon fresh ginger into the sauce and swap the rice vinegar for fresh lime juice for a brighter, zippier glaze.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerate the chicken and sauce together in an airtight container for up to 4 days. Reheat in a covered skillet over medium-low heat with 1 to 2 tablespoons water for 5 to 7 minutes, or microwave in 1-minute bursts, since the glaze thickens in the fridge. It freezes well for up to 3 months; thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Recipe FAQ

Can I use bone-in thighs?

Yes, but they need longer. Sear skin side down for 8 to 10 minutes, then cover and simmer in the sauce for 15 to 20 minutes, until they reach 175°F (80°C), before adding the slurry.

Why is my sauce not thickening?

It likely did not come to a real simmer after the slurry went in. Cornstarch only thickens near boiling, so turn up the heat until the sauce bubbles steadily for a full minute.

Can I make it less sweet?

Cut the honey to 1/4 cup and add an extra 1 teaspoon rice vinegar. The glaze will be slightly thinner, so simmer it an extra minute.
