Creamy Garlic Pasta

Fettuccine tossed in a glossy Parmesan cream sauce built on butter-toasted garlic, done in the time it takes to boil water.

From our kitchen

We reach for this on nights when the fridge is thin and patience is thinner. Buy a real wedge of Parmesan and grate it fine, because pre-shredded cheese carries anti-caking starch that keeps it from melting smoothly. A green salad with a sharp lemon dressing is all it needs alongside.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

What if the best pasta sauce in your repertoire took fewer ingredients than a grocery list for toast? Six cloves of garlic, a cup of cream and a handful of good Parmesan are all it asks for, and the whole thing comes together in the same 15 minutes the fettuccine spends in the pot.

The quiet hero is the starchy pasta water. The starch released by the noodles acts as an emulsifier, helping the butterfat in the cream and the proteins in the cheese bind into one glossy sauce instead of separating into a greasy puddle. Splitting the garlic two ways helps too: the sliced pieces turn sweet and nutty, while the minced ones keep a sharper bite.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Boil and salt the pasta

    Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil over high heat and add the salt. Cook the fettuccine 1 minute less than the package directs, so it can finish in the sauce without going soft. Before draining, scoop out 1 cup of the cloudy cooking water and set it by the stove.

    Tip: Put a heatproof measuring cup in the colander as a reminder. It is the single easiest way to never pour the pasta water down the drain again.
  2. 2

    Toast the garlic gently

    While the pasta cooks, melt the butter in a large skillet over medium-low heat until it stops foaming. Add the sliced and minced garlic and cook for 2 to 3 minutes, stirring, until it smells nutty and the slices are just turning pale gold at the edges. Pull back the heat if it browns fast, since dark garlic turns bitter.

  3. 3

    Simmer the cream

    Pour in the cream and bring it to a gentle simmer, with small, lazy bubbles around the edges. Let it bubble softly for 3 minutes, until it thickens just enough to coat the back of a spoon.

  4. 4

    Melt in the Parmesan

    Turn the heat to low and stir in the Parmesan a handful at a time, waiting for each addition to melt before adding the next. The sauce should turn smooth and pale gold. Gentle heat matters here, because boiling makes the cheese proteins seize into grainy clumps.

    Tip: If the sauce does turn grainy, take the pan off the heat and whisk in a splash of hot pasta water. It often brings a slightly broken sauce back together.
  5. 5

    Toss with pasta water

    Add the drained pasta to the skillet with 1/2 cup of the reserved pasta water. Toss with tongs over low heat for 1 to 2 minutes, until the sauce turns glossy and clings to every strand. If it looks tight or sticky, loosen it with more pasta water a splash at a time.

  6. 6

    Season and serve

    Finish with plenty of black pepper, taste for salt, and scatter over the parsley and extra Parmesan. Serve right away on warm plates, since cream sauces thicken as they cool.

Tips for success

  • Grate the Parmesan on the fine side of a box grater or a rasp. Fine shreds melt almost instantly on low heat, while coarse ones need more time and more heat, which is how sauces go grainy.
  • Warm your serving bowls with a ladle of hot pasta water while you toss the noodles. A warm bowl keeps the sauce loose to the last bite.
  • Reserve the full cup of pasta water even though the recipe calls for half. Cream sauces tighten quickly, and having extra on hand lets you rescue a thick sauce at the table.

Make it your own

Variations

Lemon and pepperAdd the finely grated zest of 1 lemon with the Parmesan and a squeeze of juice at the end. Go heavier on the black pepper to balance the richness.
Add a proteinTop each bowl with sliced grilled chicken, seared shrimp or crisp pancetta. Cook the protein first and keep it warm so the pasta timing stays the same.
Greens stirred inToss 2 big handfuls of baby spinach into the sauce with the pasta. It wilts in about a minute, so you may need an extra splash of pasta water.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for up to 2 days. Reheat in a skillet over low heat with a splash of milk or water, stirring constantly until the sauce turns creamy again. Microwaving tends to split the sauce, so the stovetop is worth the extra minute.

Recipe FAQ

Can I use milk instead of heavy cream?

Whole milk works, but the sauce will be thinner and more likely to curdle. Whisk 1 teaspoon of cornstarch into the cold milk before adding it, and keep it at a bare simmer.

Why did my sauce turn grainy or oily?

The heat was likely too high when the cheese went in, or you used pre-shredded Parmesan. Keep the pan on low, add the cheese in handfuls, and loosen with hot pasta water to bring it back.

Can I make it ahead?

It is best right away, since the sauce thickens as it sits. You can grate the cheese and slice the garlic up to a day ahead and keep them covered in the fridge.
