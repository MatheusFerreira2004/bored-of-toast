Creamy Mushroom Risotto

Silky, parmesan-rich risotto loaded with deeply browned mushrooms, fresh thyme, and a splash of white wine for cozy nights.

From our kitchen

We treat risotto night as an excuse to pour a small glass of the cooking wine and stand at the stove talking. The stirring is not constant, we promise. Our one rule is to brown the mushrooms separately, because pale mushrooms make a sad risotto.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Good risotto is creamy without any cream, and the secret is patience plus starchy arborio rice. Warm broth goes in a ladle at a time, and gentle stirring coaxes the starch out of the rice until it turns silky and loose.

We brown the mushrooms first in a hot pan so they get meaty and golden, then fold most of them back in at the end. A handful of parmesan and a knob of butter finish it off, and fall dinner is served.

Why you'll love it

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Warm the broth

    If using porcini, soak them in 1 cup of hot water for 10 minutes, then lift them out, chop finely, and pour the soaking liquid into a saucepan with the broth, leaving any grit behind. Bring the broth to a bare simmer over low heat and keep it warm.

    Tip: Cold broth shocks the rice and slows cooking, so keeping it hot really matters.
  2. 2

    Brown the mushrooms

    Heat the olive oil and 1 tablespoon butter in a large, heavy pot over medium-high heat. Add the cremini in an even layer and cook without stirring for 3 minutes, then stir and cook 4 to 5 minutes more until deep golden and any liquid has evaporated. Season with the salt, stir in the chopped porcini, and transfer everything to a bowl.

  3. 3

    Toast the rice

    Lower the heat to medium and melt 1 tablespoon butter in the same pot. Cook the onion for 4 to 5 minutes until soft and translucent, add the garlic for 30 seconds, then stir in the rice and toast for 2 minutes until the edges of the grains look translucent.

  4. 4

    Add the wine

    Pour in the wine and stir until it is almost fully absorbed, about 1 to 2 minutes. The sharp alcohol smell should fade before you add any broth.

  5. 5

    Ladle in the broth

    Add the broth one ladle (about 3/4 cup) at a time with the thyme, stirring often and waiting until each addition is mostly absorbed before adding the next. Keep it at a lively simmer, and after 18 to 22 minutes the rice should be creamy and tender with a slight bite in the center.

    Tip: You may not need every drop of broth. Start tasting the rice at 16 minutes.
  6. 6

    Finish and rest

    Take the pot off the heat and stir in most of the mushrooms, the Parmesan, and the last tablespoon of butter vigorously for 30 seconds until glossy. Season with salt and pepper, add a splash more broth if it looks stiff, and let it sit for 1 minute.

  7. 7

    Serve right away

    Spoon the risotto onto warm plates, where it should spread gently rather than hold a stiff mound. Top with the reserved mushrooms, parsley, and extra Parmesan.

Tips for success

  • Use a wide, heavy pot like a Dutch oven so the rice cooks in a shallow layer and absorbs broth evenly.
  • Do not rinse the rice. The surface starch is exactly what makes risotto creamy.
  • Aim for a texture Italians call all'onda, meaning wavy. It should slowly flow when you tilt the plate.

Make it your own

Variations

Butternut squashReplace half the mushrooms with 2 cups of small roasted butternut squash cubes, folded in at the end.
Wild mushroom mixUse a mix of shiitake, oyster, and chanterelle mushrooms for more texture and a deeper, woodsy flavor.
Lemon and peasStir in 1 cup of thawed frozen peas and the zest of a lemon during the last 3 minutes for a brighter bowl.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days, knowing the texture will firm up as it cools. Reheat in a saucepan over low heat with a few splashes of broth, or shape cold risotto into patties and pan-fry them until crisp.

Recipe FAQ

Can I make risotto without wine?

Yes. Replace the wine with an equal amount of broth plus 1 teaspoon of lemon juice or white wine vinegar for a little acidity.

Can I use a different rice?

Carnaroli and Vialone Nano both work beautifully. Long-grain rice like jasmine or basmati does not release enough starch to get creamy.

Do I really have to stir the whole time?

No. Stirring every 30 seconds or so is plenty. Constant stirring is a myth, but walking away for 10 minutes will scorch the bottom.
