Creamy Mushroom Risotto

Arborio rice stirred slowly into creamy, glossy risotto, deepened with porcini and crowned with deeply browned cremini and Parmesan.

Prep15 min
Cook35 min
Total50 min
Serves4

From our kitchen

Risotto is one of the best reasons we know to stay near the stove with a glass of the white wine you're cooking with. Brown the mushrooms separately and hard, because pale, steamed mushrooms add little flavor. A crisp green salad and the rest of that bottle round out the meal.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Risotto has a reputation for being fussy, and it's mostly undeserved. You don't need to stir nonstop, you just need to stay nearby, keep the broth hot, and add it a ladle at a time.

The creaminess comes from the rice itself, not from cream. Arborio grains are rich in amylopectin, a starch that washes off the surface as you stir and thickens the broth into a silky sauce. Browning the cremini hard on their own first builds savory depth, and the porcini soaking liquid adds a woodsy backbone that plain broth can't match.

Why you'll love it

Creamy without cream

Starch released from the arborio rice makes the sauce silky on its own.

Double mushroom depth

Dried porcini flavor the broth while browned cremini bring meaty texture.

Vegetarian main course

Vegetable broth and a rennet-free cheese keep it meat-free but hearty.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Soak porcini, heat broth

    If using porcini, soak them in 1 cup of hot water for 10 minutes until soft and pliable, then lift them out, chop finely, and pour the soaking liquid into a saucepan with the broth, leaving any grit in the bottom of the bowl. Bring the broth to a bare simmer over low heat and keep it steaming.

    Tip: Hot broth matters. Cold broth drops the pot temperature with each ladle, which slows cooking and makes the rice gummy on the outside before the center softens.
  2. 2

    Brown the mushrooms

    Heat the olive oil and 1 tablespoon of butter in a large, heavy pot over medium-high heat until the butter foams. Spread the cremini in an even layer and leave them alone for 3 minutes, then stir and cook 4 to 5 minutes more, until deep golden, smelling nutty, and the pan is dry. Season with the salt, stir in the chopped porcini, and transfer everything to a bowl.

    Tip: Hold off on salting until the mushrooms are browned. Salt added early draws out water and they steam instead of sear.
  3. 3

    Soften onion, toast rice

    Lower the heat to medium and melt 1 tablespoon of butter in the same pot. Cook the onion for 4 to 5 minutes until soft and translucent, add the garlic for 30 seconds until fragrant, then stir in the rice. Toast for 2 minutes, until the edges of the grains look translucent and you hear a faint crackle.

  4. 4

    Deglaze with wine

    Pour in the wine, which will hiss and steam, and stir, scraping up the browned bits, until it is almost fully absorbed, about 1 to 2 minutes. Wait until the sharp alcohol smell fades before adding any broth.

  5. 5

    Add broth gradually

    Add the hot broth one ladle (about 3/4 cup) at a time along with the thyme, stirring often and waiting until each addition is mostly absorbed before adding the next. Keep it at a lively simmer. After 18 to 22 minutes the rice should be creamy and tender, with a slight bite in the center.

    Tip: You may not need every drop of broth. Start tasting at 16 minutes, and stop adding broth once the grains are tender with just a hint of firmness.
  6. 6

    Beat in cheese and butter

    Take the pot off the heat and vigorously stir in most of the mushrooms, the Parmesan, and the last tablespoon of butter for 30 seconds, until glossy and flowing. Season with salt and pepper, add a splash more broth if it looks stiff, and let it rest for 1 minute.

  7. 7

    Serve right away

    Spoon the risotto onto warm plates, where it should spread gently into a loose wave rather than hold a stiff mound. Top with the reserved mushrooms, parsley and extra Parmesan.

Tips for success

  • Don't rinse the rice. The surface starch you would wash away is exactly what thickens the risotto.
  • Stop cooking when the risotto looks slightly looser than you want. It keeps absorbing liquid on the plate and thickens within a minute or two.
  • To get a head start for guests, cook the risotto through the wine and 2 ladles of broth, spread it on a sheet pan to cool, and refrigerate for up to 1 day. Finish with hot broth in about 10 minutes.

Make it your own

Variations

Peas and lemonStir in 1 cup of frozen peas during the last 3 minutes of cooking and finish with the grated zest of 1 lemon.
Wine-freeReplace the wine with the same amount of broth plus 1 teaspoon of lemon juice or white wine vinegar for brightness.
Chicken broth versionUse low-sodium chicken broth for a richer, more savory result. The recipe will no longer be vegetarian, and you may need less salt.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days; the texture will firm up as it cools. Reheat in a saucepan over low heat with a few splashes of broth, stirring until creamy. Cold risotto also makes excellent patties, pan-fried in oil until crisp and golden, about 3 minutes per side.

Recipe FAQ

Can I use another rice instead of arborio?

Carnaroli or vialone nano both work, and carnaroli holds its shape especially well. Long-grain or basmati rice won't release enough starch to turn creamy.

Why is my risotto gluey or mushy?

It was likely overcooked, stirred with cold broth, or left to sit too long before serving. Start tasting at 16 minutes and serve it the moment it is loose and glossy.

Do I really have to stir the whole time?

No. Stir often, every minute or so, to release starch and prevent sticking, but you can step away briefly between ladles.
