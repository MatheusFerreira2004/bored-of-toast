Smashed Avocado Toast with Jammy Eggs

Olive oil-fried sourdough, lemony chili-flecked avocado and a soft-centered egg with a yolk the texture of warm marmalade.

From our kitchen

We like this best on a slow weekend morning, with strong coffee and nothing else on the plate. The one thing not to skip is frying the bread in olive oil rather than using the toaster. It gives you a crunchy, savory crust that holds up under the avocado instead of going limp.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Picture a thick slice of sourdough, crackling at the edges from a hot pan, spread with lemony avocado and topped with a halved egg whose yolk is set like fudge at the rim and glossy in the middle. It takes 20 minutes and a timer. That is it.

The jammy yolk comes down to precise timing and a cold start. Egg whites set at around 180°F while yolks thicken between roughly 150°F and 158°F, so a short, exact boil gets the white firm while the yolk stays in that soft, spoonable zone. The ice bath matters just as much: it stops carryover heat from cooking the yolk further, and the quick temperature drop makes the membrane pull away from the shell so the eggs peel cleanly.

Why you'll love it

Reliable jammy eggs

A 6 1/2 minute boil and ice bath gives the same soft yolk every time.

Toast that stays crunchy

Pan-frying in olive oil builds a sturdy crust that holds the avocado.

Seasoned all the way

Lemon, salt and chili go into the avocado, not just on top.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Boil the water

    Fill a small saucepan with enough water to cover the eggs by an inch and bring it to a full rolling boil over high heat. While it heats, fill a bowl with ice and cold water and set it beside the stove.

  2. 2

    Cook the jammy eggs

    Lower the cold eggs into the boiling water with a slotted spoon and start a timer for 6 minutes 30 seconds. Turn the heat down slightly so the water holds a steady, lively simmer instead of a violent boil, which can knock the eggs into the pan and crack their shells.

    Tip: For a runnier yolk, go 6 minutes. For a firmer, fudgy yolk, go 7 minutes. Stick with the same pan and number of eggs and your timing will stay consistent.
  3. 3

    Shock and peel

    When the timer goes off, move the eggs straight into the ice bath and leave them for at least 3 minutes, until they feel cool to the touch. Tap each egg all over on the counter to create a web of fine cracks, then peel under a trickle of cold water, starting at the wide end where the air pocket is.

    Tip: Slightly older eggs peel more easily than very fresh ones. As eggs age, their pH rises and the white clings less to the inner membrane.
  4. 4

    Smash the avocado

    While the eggs chill, halve and pit the avocado and scoop the flesh into a bowl. Add the lemon juice, salt and red pepper flakes and mash with a fork until mostly smooth with a few chunky bits left for texture. Taste it: if it seems flat, add another pinch of salt or squeeze of lemon.

  5. 5

    Toast the bread

    Heat the olive oil in a skillet over medium heat until it shimmers. Add the bread and fry for 2 to 3 minutes per side, pressing gently with a spatula, until it is deep golden, sizzling and crisp outside but still slightly chewy in the middle.

  6. 6

    Assemble and serve

    Spread the avocado thickly over the warm toast, right to the edges so every bite gets some. Halve the eggs lengthwise and nestle them on top, then finish with flaky salt, black pepper, extra chili flakes, a thin drizzle of olive oil and chives or microgreens if using. Eat right away while the toast still crunches.

Tips for success

  • Cook the eggs straight from the fridge. The timing here assumes a cold egg, and a room-temperature egg will overcook by the same clock.
  • To tell if an avocado is ripe, flick off the little stem cap. If it comes away easily and the flesh beneath is green, it is ready; brown underneath means it is overripe.
  • Make the eggs up to 3 days ahead and keep them peeled in the fridge. Warm them for 1 minute in a bowl of hot tap water before slicing so the yolks loosen and are not fridge-cold.
  • Small-portion tip: serve half a portion and save the rest for later. A half bowl still counts.

Make it your own

Variations

Everything bagel styleSkip the red pepper flakes on top and sprinkle the eggs with 1 teaspoon of everything bagel seasoning, going lighter on the flaky salt.
Add feta and tomatoTop with crumbled feta and a few sliced cherry tomatoes. Note that the feta makes it no longer dairy-free.
Gluten-free toastUse thick slices of gluten-free bread and fry them over slightly lower heat, since they tend to brown faster than sourdough.
Make it gentlerUse one slice instead of two and keep the egg soft and jammy.
Protein boostSpread a spoonful of cottage cheese under the avocado.

Storage & reheating

Avocado toast is best eaten right away, since the bread softens and the avocado browns within minutes. Peeled jammy eggs keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days; warm them in hot tap water for a minute before serving. Leftover smashed avocado keeps for 1 day in a small container with plastic wrap pressed directly onto its surface to block air.

Recipe FAQ

Why won't my eggs peel cleanly?

Very fresh eggs stick to their shells, so use eggs that are at least a week old if you can. Skipping or rushing the ice bath also makes peeling harder.

How do I keep the avocado from turning brown?

The lemon juice slows browning by lowering the pH, which slows the enzyme responsible. Pressing plastic wrap onto the surface helps even more by keeping out oxygen.

Can I use a toaster instead of a skillet?

Yes. Toast the bread until well browned, then brush it with the olive oil. It will be a bit drier and less crunchy than pan-fried, but still good.
