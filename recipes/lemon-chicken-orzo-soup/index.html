Lemon Chicken Orzo Soup

Tender shredded chicken, silky orzo and wilted spinach in a garlicky broth, finished with a big hit of lemon and fresh dill.

From our kitchen

We would happily bring a pot of this to a friend with a cold, or make it on a gray Sunday to eat all week. Add the lemon off the heat and taste as you go; the soup should taste noticeably bright, not just a little tart. Serve it with crusty bread for dunking.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Most chicken soups are cozy but a bit sleepy. This one wakes up at the finish line with lemon zest, plenty of juice, and a handful of dill, which turns a humble pot of chicken and pasta into something you want to keep eating straight from the ladle.

Poaching the thighs right in the broth does double duty: the chicken cooks gently, and its juices and collagen enrich the soup as it simmers. Keep the heat low, since meat proteins tighten and squeeze out moisture when they are boiled hard, which is what makes chicken stringy. The lemon goes in last, off the heat, because long cooking dulls the bright citrus aromas and can turn the juice bitter.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Soften the vegetables

    Heat the olive oil in a large Dutch oven over medium heat until it shimmers. Add the onion, carrots and celery with a pinch of salt and cook for 6 to 8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onion is translucent and the carrots begin to soften. Stir in the garlic and oregano and cook for 1 minute, until the kitchen smells fragrant.

  2. 2

    Poach the chicken

    Pour in the broth, add the bay leaves, salt and pepper, and nestle the chicken into the liquid. Bring to a boil over medium-high heat, then lower to a gentle simmer, partially cover and cook for 15 to 18 minutes, until the thickest part of the chicken registers 165°F (74°C).

    Tip: Keep it at a lazy simmer with small bubbles breaking the surface. A hard boil makes the chicken tough and the broth cloudy.
  3. 3

    Shred the chicken

    Move the chicken to a cutting board and let it cool for 5 minutes, just enough to handle. Shred it into bite-sized pieces with two forks, pulling along the grain. Fish out the bay leaves and discard them.

  4. 4

    Cook the orzo

    Bring the broth back to a simmer over medium heat and stir in the orzo. Cook for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring every couple of minutes so it does not stick and scorch on the bottom, until it is tender with a slight bite in the center.

    Tip: The orzo keeps drinking broth as it sits, so pull it off the heat while it still has a little chew.
  5. 5

    Add lemon and greens

    Turn off the heat and stir in the shredded chicken, spinach, lemon zest and lemon juice. Let the pot sit for 1 to 2 minutes, until the spinach has wilted to dark green. Stir in the dill, then taste and add more salt, pepper or lemon until the broth tastes bright and well seasoned.

    Tip: Add the lemon juice in stages. Start with about two thirds, taste, and add the rest if the soup still tastes flat. Salt and acid work together, so if it tastes sour but thin, add salt rather than more lemon.
  6. 6

    Serve

    Ladle the soup into bowls and top with extra dill and a crack of black pepper. Serve with lemon wedges and crusty bread on the side.

Tips for success

  • Thighs are more forgiving than breasts. Their extra fat and connective tissue keep them juicy even if they simmer a few minutes too long, while breasts dry out quickly past 165°F.
  • If you plan to eat the soup over several days, cook the orzo separately in salted water and add it to each bowl. That way the pasta does not swell and soak up all the broth in the fridge.
  • Zest the lemons before you juice them. It is much easier on a whole lemon, and the zest carries most of the fragrant oils, so do not skip it.

Make it your own

Variations

Greek avgolemono styleWhisk 2 eggs with the lemon juice, slowly whisk in 2 cups of the hot broth to temper, then stir the mixture back into the pot off the heat for a creamy, silky soup.
Use rotisserie chickenSkip the poaching and simmer the broth with the bay leaves for 10 minutes, then add 4 cups of shredded rotisserie chicken with the spinach.
Swap the pastaUse 1 cup of long-grain rice instead of orzo and simmer it for 15 to 18 minutes, or use a gluten-free small pasta and follow the package time.

Storage & reheating

Refrigerate the soup in airtight containers for up to 4 days. The orzo absorbs broth overnight, so reheat it in a saucepan over medium heat with an extra 1/2 to 1 cup of broth, stirring until steaming. To freeze, make the soup without orzo and freeze for up to 3 months, then cook fresh orzo when you reheat it.

Recipe FAQ

Can I use chicken breasts instead of thighs?

Yes. Start checking them at 12 minutes and pull them as soon as they hit 165°F (74°C), since breasts dry out faster than thighs.

Why did my soup turn thick and stodgy?

The orzo kept absorbing liquid as it sat. Stir in more broth or water until it loosens, then re-season with salt and lemon.

Can I make it ahead?

Yes. Make the soup through the chicken step up to 2 days ahead, then reheat, cook the orzo and finish with lemon, spinach and dill just before serving.
