Easy Apple Crisp

Soft, cinnamon-spiced apples bubbling under a craggy oat and brown sugar topping with big, buttery clusters worth fighting over.

From our kitchen

We like this warm, about 30 minutes out of the oven, with vanilla ice cream melting into the juices. Do not skip the mix of tart and sweet apples. Granny Smiths keep their shape and bring acidity, while Honeycrisp adds sweetness, and together they taste far more interesting than either alone.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

Apple crisp is the most forgiving dessert in the fall baking lineup. No crust to roll, no lattice to weave, just sliced fruit in a dish and a topping you rub together with your fingers. It still delivers everything you want from an apple pie: syrupy spiced fruit and a golden, crunchy lid.

Cold butter is what makes the topping crisp rather than cakey. When you rub it into the flour and oats, the butter stays in separate little pieces, and in the oven the water in those pieces turns to steam while the fat browns the sugar around it, leaving crunchy, craggy clusters. The spoonful of flour in the filling matters too: it thickens the apple juices as they bubble so you get a glossy sauce instead of a watery pool.

Why you'll love it

Big crunchy clusters

Cold butter rubbed in by hand bakes into a craggy, golden topping.

Thick, glossy filling

A little flour thickens the juices so nothing turns watery.

Easy to make ahead

Assemble it a day early and bake straight from the fridge.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Heat the oven

    Set a rack in the center of the oven and preheat to 375°F (190°C). Lightly butter a 9-inch square or 2-quart baking dish and place it on a rimmed baking sheet to catch any drips.

  2. 2

    Make the filling

    In a large bowl, toss the sliced apples with the brown sugar, lemon juice, flour, cinnamon, nutmeg and salt until every slice is evenly coated. Spread them into the baking dish in an even layer, pressing down gently to close up any big gaps so the fruit cooks evenly.

    Tip: Slice the apples an even 1/4 inch thick. Uneven slices cook at different rates, so you end up with some mushy pieces and some that are still crunchy.
  3. 3

    Rub in the topping

    In the same bowl, whisk together the flour, oats, brown sugar, cinnamon and salt. Add the cold butter cubes and rub them in with your fingertips for 2 to 3 minutes, until the mixture looks like damp sand with some pea-sized clumps, then stir in the nuts if using. Work quickly so your warm hands do not melt the butter.

    Tip: Squeeze a few handfuls together to make bigger clusters. They bake into the best crunchy bits.
  4. 4

    Top the apples

    Scatter the topping evenly over the apples, all the way to the edges of the dish. Leave it loose and craggy rather than pressing it down, since a loose layer lets steam escape and bakes up crisper.

  5. 5

    Bake until bubbling

    Bake for 40 to 45 minutes, until the topping is deep golden brown and the juices are bubbling thickly around the edges. A knife should slide easily into the apples in the center. If the top browns before the apples are tender, tent it loosely with foil for the last 10 minutes.

    Tip: Look for slow, thick bubbles, not just thin juice around the edges. The flour needs to boil to fully thicken the filling, so thin bubbles mean it needs more time.
  6. 6

    Rest and serve

    Cool the crisp on a wire rack for at least 15 minutes so the juices thicken and the topping firms up. Serve warm with a scoop of vanilla ice cream or a spoonful of whipped cream.

Tips for success

  • Freeze the butter cubes for 10 minutes before rubbing them in, especially in a warm kitchen. Colder butter stays in distinct pieces longer, which means crisper topping.
  • Make a double batch of topping and freeze half in a zip-top bag for up to 3 months. Sprinkle it straight from frozen over any fruit, adding a few minutes to the bake time.
  • If your crisp comes out soupy, the apples were likely very juicy or it was cut too soon. Next time add an extra tablespoon of flour to the filling and let it rest the full 15 minutes.

Make it your own

Variations

Apple and berrySwap 1 pound of the apples for 3 cups of blueberries or blackberries and add 1 extra tablespoon of flour to thicken the extra juice.
Pear and gingerUse firm Bosc pears instead of apples and add 1 teaspoon of ground ginger to the filling.
Gluten-free crispSwap the flour in both the filling and topping for a 1:1 gluten-free baking blend and use certified gluten-free oats.

Storage & reheating

Cover the crisp and keep it at room temperature for 1 day or in the fridge for up to 4 days. Reheat individual portions on a baking sheet in a 350°F (175°C) oven for about 10 minutes to bring back the crunch; the microwave warms it faster but softens the topping. You can also freeze the baked, cooled crisp, well wrapped, for up to 3 months and reheat it from thawed.

Recipe FAQ

What are the best apples for apple crisp?

Firm, slightly tart apples work best because they hold their shape. We like Granny Smith mixed with Honeycrisp, Braeburn, Pink Lady or Jonagold. Avoid soft varieties like McIntosh, which turn to applesauce.

What is the difference between a crisp and a crumble?

A crisp traditionally includes oats in the topping, which bake up crunchy. A crumble usually has a streusel of just flour, sugar and butter.

Can I make apple crisp ahead of time?

Yes. Assemble it up to 1 day ahead, cover and refrigerate, then add 5 to 10 minutes to the baking time. You can also bake it fully and rewarm it in the oven before serving.
