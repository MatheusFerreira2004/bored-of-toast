Creamy Tomato Basil Soup

Canned whole tomatoes simmer down with sweet onion and garlic, then get blended silky with fresh basil and a splash of cream.

VGF
Prep10 min
Cook25 min
Total35 min
Serves4

From our kitchen

Honestly, good canned tomatoes beat most supermarket fresh ones for this soup, and we would not bother waiting for summer. Serve it with a grilled cheese or thick slices of buttered toast for dunking. Do not skip the uncovered simmer, since that is where the flavor concentrates.

The Bored of Toast kitchen ♥

A pot of velvety tomato soup, steaming and brick-orange with a white swirl of cream on top, is one of the best things you can make from two cans. This version takes 35 minutes, and most of that is hands-off simmering. It is the kind of bowl that makes a rainy weeknight feel like a plan.

Two small moves do a lot of work. A teaspoon of sugar does not make the soup sweet; it softens the sharp acidity of canned tomatoes so their fruity flavor comes forward. And the cream goes in over low heat at the end, because boiling dairy in an acidic soup can make its proteins tighten and curdle into grainy flecks.

Why you'll love it

Pantry-friendly ingredients

Canned tomatoes, broth, an onion and garlic are all you need, plus a handful of basil.

Deep flavor, 25 minutes

An uncovered simmer evaporates water and concentrates the tomatoes without hours on the stove.

Silky without flour

Blending the tomatoes and onion gives body naturally, and just 1/3 cup cream makes it velvety.

Instructions

Tap a step number to mark it as done.

  1. 1

    Soften the onion and garlic

    Heat the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat until it shimmers. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook, stirring now and then, for 6 to 8 minutes, until soft, translucent and sweet-smelling but not browned. Add the garlic and stir for 1 minute, just until fragrant.

    Tip: The pinch of salt pulls moisture from the onion so it softens faster and does not scorch.
  2. 2

    Simmer uncovered

    Pour in the tomatoes with their juices and crush them against the side of the pot with a wooden spoon. Stir in the broth and sugar and bring to a boil over medium-high heat. Lower to a steady simmer and cook uncovered for 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the soup has reduced slightly and the raw, tinny edge of the tomatoes is gone.

    Tip: If the tomatoes still taste sharp after simmering, add another pinch of sugar or a small knob of butter. Both round out the acidity.
  3. 3

    Blend with the basil

    Take the pot off the heat and add the basil leaves. Blend with an immersion blender for 1 to 2 minutes, until completely smooth and flecked with green. If using a countertop blender, fill it no more than halfway, work in batches and leave the lid vent open under a folded towel so steam can escape.

  4. 4

    Stir in the cream

    Return the pot to low heat and stir in the heavy cream until the soup turns a soft, even orange. Warm gently for 2 minutes without letting it boil. Taste and season with salt and pepper; canned tomatoes vary a lot, so trust your tongue.

    Tip: Keep the heat low once the cream is in. A rolling boil can make the dairy split and look grainy.
  5. 5

    Ladle and serve

    Ladle the soup into warm bowls and swirl a spoonful of extra cream across each. Finish with a few torn basil leaves and plenty of cracked black pepper. Serve hot with thick slices of crusty bread for dunking.

Tips for success

  • Buy whole peeled tomatoes rather than crushed or diced. Diced tomatoes are often treated with calcium chloride to keep their shape, which means they resist breaking down and blend less smoothly.
  • For a deeper, roasted flavor, let the onion go an extra 3 to 4 minutes until lightly golden before adding the garlic. The browning adds savory sweetness without any extra ingredients.
  • Make the soup through the blending step up to 2 days ahead and refrigerate. Add the cream only when you reheat it, so it tastes fresh and there is no risk of it splitting twice.

Make it your own

Variations

Make it dairy-freeSwap the heavy cream for 1/3 cup full-fat canned coconut milk. It adds a faint sweetness that works well with the basil.
Add roasted red pepperBlend in 1 cup jarred roasted red peppers, drained, with the basil for a smokier, sweeter soup. You may want an extra 1/2 cup broth to thin it.
Give it a little heatAdd 1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes with the garlic, and finish each bowl with a drizzle of chili oil instead of cream.

Storage & reheating

Cool the soup and refrigerate in airtight containers for up to 4 days. Reheat gently in a saucepan over medium-low heat, stirring often, or microwave in 1-minute bursts. It freezes well for up to 3 months, though it is best frozen before the cream goes in; thaw overnight in the fridge and stir in the cream as it reheats.

Recipe FAQ

Can I use fresh tomatoes?

Yes, use about 4 pounds of ripe tomatoes, cored and roughly chopped. Simmer 25 to 30 minutes instead of 15 so they break down fully, and add broth only as needed, since fresh tomatoes release a lot of liquid.

Why did my soup curdle?

The cream probably went into soup that was boiling, or it was very cold. Temper it by stirring a ladle of hot soup into the cream first, then add it over low heat and do not let it boil.

How can I make it thicker?

Simmer it uncovered for another 5 to 10 minutes before blending, or use 1 1/2 cups broth instead of 2. You can also blend in a slice of crustless bread, a classic trick that adds body.
